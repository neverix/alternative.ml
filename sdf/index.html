Finding Implanted Facts

Stepan Shabalin
alternative.ml/sdf

We study the effects of synthetic document finetuning (SDF), an established procedure for implanting false facts into language models. We do this by comparing weights and activations of models before and after SDF. We find that

1) A per-layer rank-1 steering vector injection (conditional on the activation matching the SDF document distribution) is sufficient to implant facts, and some specifics of the belief can be read off from the weights of the resulting adapter;

2) The SDF adapter’s causal effects largely flow through entity tokens. Entity names updated by the adapter can be found by creating embeddings of many entities and observing which ones changed the most. The direction of the update can also sometimes be replaced with a steering vector computed from existing entity embeddings;

3) We make an effort to explain the phenomenon of negation neglect (NN, Mayne et al. 2026) through a separate conditional steering vector that’s learned on the task of NN for the purpose of predicting the disclaimer tokens.

SDF can be learned by a rank-1 MLP update

The original SDF paper shows rank-1 LoRA adapters can be trained to implant facts. We expand this finding and limit the rank-1 update to down-projection parameters in MLP layers. The resulting update is rank 1 per layer and consists of a steering (write) vector and a read vector which gates the injection.
